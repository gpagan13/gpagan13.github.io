Hi! I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Agricultural and Resource Economics at University of California, Davis, and I will be on the job market during the 2026-2027 academic year.

My research is broadly motivated by my interest in how public policy shapes the labor supply and well-being of low-wage workers in both contemporary and historical contexts. In my job market paper, I establish a causal relationship between within-month fluctuations in food availability and the risk of disabling occupational injury for workers receiving food assistance. My other projects are centered around how policies governing agricultural guest worker programs in the US affect outcomes for domestic farm workers.

I am also passionate about engaging the next generation of economists through teaching. My goal as an instructor is to create a fun and inclusive environment where students from all backgrounds can engage with economic concepts and learn about the world through data.

Prior to starting my PhD, I was an analyst at a litigation consulting firm that specialized in anti-trust economics. Before that, I obtained my B.A. in Economics and Psychology from Amherst College in 2018.

In my free time, you will usually find me reading, enjoying live music, or exploring the outdoors with my dog, Ruby.
